Bioinformatics & Computational Biology

I'm a bioinformatician interested in understanding how molecular changes contribute to neurodegenerative diseases, particularly Alzheimer's disease and ALS/TDP-43 dysfunction. I work with genomic and transcriptomic data to study disease-associated genes, RNA, and molecular networks, and I’m especially interested in using computational methods to answer biological questions.

I have worked with research groups at GCUF, the University of Idaho, and UCL’s Fratta Lab on projects involving graph neural networks, RNA-seq, cryptic circRNAs, copy-number variation, and disease-gene databases.

Explainable GNN Framework for Alzheimer's

An explainable GraphSAGE-based framework was applied to Alzheimer's disease network data to surface interpretable gene-level signals. The diagram shows how the top-ranked genes fit into one mechanism: oxidative stress (GAPDH, MDH1), chaperone overload (HSPA8, HSP90AB1), failed tau clearance (RNF216), tau fibrillation, and loss of SNAP25 at the synapse. It was included to show that the model output is biologically meaningful, not only a score.

Cryptic circRNAs in TDP-43 Dysfunction

UCL Fratta Lab project on cryptic circular RNAs that appear when TDP-43 loses its function. RNA-seq from muscle biopsies is used to find circRNAs that could serve as stable biomarkers.

Neurological Disease CNV Database

A personal, curated database that maps neurological disease genes to CNV type (deletion or duplication), cytoband, and genomic coordinates. It is meant as a quick lookup resource for disease research.

Research

Jul 2026 – Oct 2026

Fratta Lab

UCL Queen Square Institute of Neurology

Remote Research Intern · Supervisor: Dr. Pietro Fratta

  • Cryptic circRNA formation caused by TDP-43 dysfunction is being studied in Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM), using RNA-seq from IBM and control muscle biopsies.
  • CircRNAs are detected through back-splice junctions, and IBM-enriched candidates are cross-checked against known TDP-43 cryptic splicing events from ALS/FTD datasets.
  • Candidates are classified as CNS-shared or muscle-specific to select targets for validation as biomarkers of TDP-43 loss of function.
Nov 2025 – Present

Department of Computer Science

University of Idaho

Remote Research Assistant · Principal Investigator: Dr. Hasan Jamil

  • An explainable Graph Neural Network (GNN) framework was developed to identify Alzheimer's disease-associated gene modules from protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks.
  • GraphSAGE and GCN models were implemented with perturbation-based explainability to rank and interpret key disease genes.
  • Top-ranked genes were validated through KEGG pathway enrichment, Gene Ontology (GO) analysis, and GWAS overlap.
Aug 2025 – Present

Integrative Omics and Molecular Modeling Lab

Department of Bioinformatics & Biotechnology, GCUF · Faisalabad, PK

Research Assistant · Principal Investigator: Dr. Muhammad Tahir ul Qamar

  • A biological database is being developed that integrates copy number variations (CNVs), epigenetic markers, and 3D structural data for neurodegenerative disease.
  • Schema and pipelines are being designed for efficient integration, retrieval, and analysis of multi-omics data.
  • The aim is a resource that supports biomarker discovery and therapeutic research in neurological disease.
Feb 2025 – Jul 2025

Biocatalysis and Protein Engineering Research Group

Department of Bioinformatics & Biotechnology, GCUF

Undergraduate Research· Principal Investigator: Dr. Muhammad Rizwan Javed

  • An integrative analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and copy number variations (CNVs) was conducted in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.
